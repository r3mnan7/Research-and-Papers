# RQ2 Screening Log (Step 1 of RQ2)

Back to [[Addressing Fear, Uncertainty, and Doubt in the Security and Safety of Artificial Intelligence Models]]

> [!note] Drafting and review
> Work in progress. Drafted with AI assistance (2026); the author's review is ongoing. See [[Methodology — Use of Generative AI]].

Grouping, paths, lights, and scope calls were produced with AI assistance under the fixed method below. Each concern links to its full attack-path note with sources.

> [!info] What this is
> An independent dataset of the AI risks people worry about, gathered from practitioner and government guidance, industry and popular risk lists, catastrophe statements, public-opinion polls, surveys of AI researchers and security practitioners, and the MIT AI Risk Repository (35 sources). Mentions naming the same thing are grouped into one concern. Every concern then goes through the same attack-path test.

## Method

1. **Harm:** state the harm the sources claim, at the severity they claim it. If sources claim very different severities, rate a baseline and a maximal path separately.
2. **Path:** list only the necessary steps, in order, always including the step where AI does what the concern says it does. Alternative routes sit inside a step.
3. **Light each step:** 🟢 happened outside a controlled test, documented by a primary source · 🟡 shown only in a controlled setting (lab, evaluation, red team, proof of concept, researcher demo) · 🔴 no demonstration found · ⚪ not yet researched. A step with alternative routes takes the light of its best-evidenced in-scope route.
    - **Allegations are not established facts.** A step that rests only on allegations (complaints, lawsuits, accusations against a named party, open investigations, settlements or consent orders without findings) is 🔴 (allegation only). What is on the record in court, or in public filings that are legally treated as facts (findings, judgments, regulator decisions, admissions), counts.
    - An incident that begins in a controlled test but reaches live systems outside it is 🟢.
    - A step that recurs across concerns uses one wording and one light everywhere (shared steps, below).
4. **Weakest link:** a path's light is its worst step.
5. **In scope (decided after the path):** A concern is In when the harm cannot happen without a failure of the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of an information system or the information it handles, including failure of the authentication, authorization, and accountability mechanisms that protect them. This follows the statutory definition of information security, 44 U.S.C. § 3552(b)(3) [\[6\]](Sources.md#^s6). Each route is tested with three questions: (1) What system or information fails? (2) Which of those properties fails? (3) Is that failure necessary on this route? A concern is In if at least one route passes and Out if none does; there is no partial verdict. Only routes that pass are analyzed, and the Light column gives their light. Every other route is named, with its light, under **Routes outside scope**, so nothing is dropped from the record.
    - **Integrity** means "guarding against improper information modification or destruction" (44 U.S.C. § 3552(b)(3)(A) [\[6\]](Sources.md#^s6); the same definition is used in FIPS 200 and SP 800-53, per NIST's glossary [\[7\]](Sources.md#^s7)). A model being wrong, with no attacker and no security control defeated, is not an integrity failure.
    - **Social engineering is an attack on authentication.** A person acting on an identity or instruction that wasn't verified counts as an authentication failure.
    - **Resilience** is read as part of availability: recovering from attacks. Safety fallbacks (e.g., emergency braking) do not count on their own.
    - A deployer's content restrictions are authorization rules, so bypassing them is a security failure.
    - Path notes that say "system control" mean authorization (an action outside what was approved) or integrity (a system altered outside its approved state).
    - **Check against the field:** the definition must include phishing, business email compromise, ransomware, credential stuffing, insider misuse, supply-chain compromise, and DDoS. If it excludes one, the definition is wrong, not the concern.
6. **On the record:** court findings, regulator final decisions, statutory or parliamentary inquiry findings, and admissions by the responsible party. A victim's own record counts when it produces evidence; claims without evidence are allegations.
