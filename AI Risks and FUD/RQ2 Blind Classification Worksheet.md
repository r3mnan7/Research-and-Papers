# RQ2 Blind Classification Worksheet

Back to [[RQ2 Risk Classification]]

Classes, reasons, and rationales reviewed and approved by the human reviewer, 2026-11-04.

**G/S** gives the screening-log concerns and catastrophe scenarios each row covers; *Privacy Risk from Data Aggregation* comes from the NIST catalogue and has no in-scope screening-log concern. **Rationale (academic)** is the formal version of each Why.

**Class** may also read *Out* (out of scope) or *Mapped* (covered by other rows, not counted). Where later reasoning replaced an earlier Why, the Why column holds the later reasoning.

Put A, B, C, or D in the **Class** column for each row. **Why** is optional; a few words helps when we compare. Don't open [[RQ2 Risk Classification]] until you're done. When you've finished, the AI assistant will then compare your classes with the current ones.

> [!tip] Quick reference
> **Classify only what's described in the row** (the in-scope route), not every way the concern could happen.
> 
> | Class | Name | Test |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | **A** | Amplified | An established attack or failure on an established target. AI changes the speed, volume, cost, or skill needed. |
> | **B** | Relocated | An established attack or failure applied to a new artifact or place (weights, embeddings, prompts, agent credentials). Existing controls apply once re-scoped. |
> | **C** | Novel, contained | Exploits a property of *learned* representations, with no conventional software flaw. Controls outside the model still bound the impact. |
> | **D** | Novel, assurance gap | Same as C, but with controls fully in place there's no way to get assurance comparable to conventional software. Residual risk remains. |
> 
> **Three questions, in order:**
> 1. Is the mechanism one security already knows from before AI? Same target, AI only makes it faster, cheaper, bigger, or easier → **A**. Same mechanism on a new AI artifact or location → **B**.
> 2. If not: does it exploit how a learned model behaves, rather than a software flaw? → **C or D**.
> 3. C or D: with every relevant control in place, can you still not get assurance against it? → **D**. Otherwise → **C**.
> 
> **Rules already decided:**
> - A model's own error (no attacker) that defeats a security control or produces an action outside what was authorized → **B**.
> - An adversary exploiting learned behavior to cause the wrong output → **C or D**.
> - An AI pursuing a goal of its own isn't an error → **D**.
> - Many models acting alike, each working as built → **A**.
> - "Novel to ML" isn't "new in 2026": adversarial examples (2013), model extraction (2016), membership inference (2017), backdoors (2017) and gradient leakage (2019) are long known, but age alone doesn't make something A or B.
> - Containment isn't prevention: a risk can be fully contained outside the model and still unpreventable inside it. That's the C/D line.
