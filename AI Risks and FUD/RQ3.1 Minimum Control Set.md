**Research question 3.1:** Across the seven general-purpose frameworks, what is the smallest set of requirements that stops each Class C and D attack path at the earliest step an established control can stop it, and what lies beyond that set?

## The One-Shot Rule

This is a minimum viable set, not a defense-in-depth design. For each path in [[RQ3 Control Coverage]], take the **earliest step whose verdict is Bounded, or Bounded within limits, by a requirement outside the model**, and keep only the requirements cited at that step. Every later bounded step on the same path is a second line of defense and is left out here; it stays in RQ3. Steps whose bound does not stop the path (legitimate access that the service sells; query budgets that raise cost without stopping the attacker; provenance that attests origin but not behavior) are passed over for the next step that does.

Two consequences follow, and two exceptions: [[G41 Physical safety (cyber-physical AI)|G41]]'s adversarial-sensor route and [[G42 CBRN information or capabilities|G42]]'s safeguard bypass have no stop inside the seven frameworks that fires on the attack itself (see their rows). For every **Class C** path the one shot lands upstream of the AI step, so the model never sees the attacker's input. For every **Class D** path nothing upstream can stop it, so the one shot is the first step after the AI step: the grant, or the isolation. That is the whole difference between the classes, stated as controls.

The assumption is [[RQ3 Control Coverage|RQ3]]'s: full implementation at the frameworks' highest stated level (CIS IG3, all 153 safeguards [\[289\]](Sources.md#^s289); ZTMM Optimal; ASVS Level 3; SP 800-53 enhancements where cited). Where a pick is above a common baseline, the level is shown.

## Two Additions From the Incident Record

Two requirements are in the minimum although neither is the earliest stopping step on any path. [[RQ4 Rogue AI Incidents]] found that they decided outcomes in real incidents where the earliest stop was missing, so the one-shot rule alone would leave an organization without them.

- **Backup and tested recovery** of whatever a model or agent can change: 53: [[NIST SP 800-53 Rev 5 Catalog#^cp-9|CP-9]], [[NIST SP 800-53 Rev 5 Catalog#^cp-10|CP-10]]; CIS: [[CIS Controls v8.1 Catalog#^cis-11-2|11.2]] (IG1), [[CIS Controls v8.1 Catalog#^cis-11-4|11.4]] (IG1), [[CIS Controls v8.1 Catalog#^cis-11-5|11.5]] (IG2); CSF: [[NIST CSF 2.0 Catalog#^pr-ds-11|PR.DS-11]]; ZTMM: Data—Data Availability. Recovery decided how much was lost in every agent deletion whose record describes recovery: Replit's platform rollback restored the database the agent deleted [\[70\]](Sources.md#^s70), [\[293\]](Sources.md#^s293), and in the four agent deletions in [[RQ4 Rogue AI Incidents|RQ4]]'s register C whose records describe recovery, it set how much was lost [\[417\]](Sources.md#^s417), [\[418\]](Sources.md#^s418), [\[420\]](Sources.md#^s420), [\[424\]](Sources.md#^s424). It acts after the harm, so it bounds the loss rather than stopping the path.
- **Revocation of an exposed credential, verified against the live credential:** 53: [[NIST SP 800-53 Rev 5 Catalog#^ia-5|IA-5]] (items d and f: procedures for compromised authenticators and for revoking them; refresh when defined events occur); CIS: [[CIS Controls v8.1 Catalog#^cis-6-2|6.2]] (IG1). In the Cline incident, the operator rotated the wrong token after a reported exposure and the exposed token remained active; that is the only causal step the operator's post-mortem establishes [\[253\]](Sources.md#^s253). Neither framework says to confirm that the revoked credential no longer works; IA-5 and CIS 6.2 require the revocation process, and the verification is the part the incident shows was missing.

## What the One Shot Leaves Out

By design, this note drops every second line [[RQ3 Control Coverage|RQ3]] found: the content filters and isolation patterns at indirect injection step 2 [\[248\]](Sources.md#^s248), [\[249\]](Sources.md#^s249); dual authorization behind an agent's grant; records kept off-box ([[NIST SP 800-53 Rev 5 Catalog#^au-9-2|AU-9(2)]], [[OWASP ASVS 5.0.0 Catalog#^asvs-16-4-3|ASVS 16.4.3]]) and detection of bypass; secrets rotation; output filtering behind source trust; anti-automation behind proofing; the tested stop. Those are defense in depth, and the paper's practice sections in [[RQ3 Control Coverage|RQ3]] are where they live. The one-shot set is what an organization must have for the containment to exist at all; the rest is what makes the containment survive a missed implementation, which, per [[RQ4 Rogue AI Incidents|RQ4]], is how every 2026 incident actually happened (with the conditional cases and the attacker-as-deployer exception in [[RQ4 Rogue AI Incidents|RQ4]]'s Remedy Check) (corrected Nov 5, 2026; see [[RQ4 Rogue AI Incidents]]).

It also leaves out, by scope rather than by design, the two routes that have no one shot in the seven frameworks: [[G42 CBRN information or capabilities|G42]]'s jailbreak route, where the output is the harm, and [[G41 Physical safety (cyber-physical AI)|G41]]'s adversarial-sensor route, where the stop that fires is a safety supervisor or fallback. The paper's Scope reads cyber-physical containment as a safety control outside information security, so neither route has a row in the minimum, and an organization running AI against physical actuators needs a safety analysis this note does not supply.

## Beyond the Minimum: What No Framework Supplies

The minimum produces containment at the earliest step a control can act. It does not act at the AI step, and [[RQ3 Control Coverage|RQ3]] names four things that no requirement in the seven frameworks supplies:

1. **In-model instruction/data separation** for the two injection rows. No method delivers it [\[27\]](Sources.md#^s27), [\[41\]](Sources.md#^s41), [\[250\]](Sources.md#^s250).
2. **Robustness testing of learned decision boundaries** beyond biometrics (adversarial inputs); certified methods cover only a small ℓ2 radius for a subset of inputs [\[255\]](Sources.md#^s255).
3. **Trigger testing of supplied models** (backdoors); finite tests cannot establish absence [\[48\]](Sources.md#^s48), and detectors state their own bounds [\[56\]](Sources.md#^s56), [\[259\]](Sources.md#^s259).
4. **Evaluation of a model's goals before deployment**, and a **stop mechanism tested to final effect**; CSF [[NIST CSF 2.0 Catalog#RS — Respond\|RS]] and [[NIST CSF 2.0 Catalog#RC — Recover\|RC]] and [[CIS Controls v8.1 Catalog#Control 17: Incident Response Management\|CIS Control 17]] cover response, none specifies the test, and the 2026 record shows the gap [\[73\]](Sources.md#^s73).

An organization that implements the minimum has bounded the harm to the grant; it has not, and cannot yet, verify the model.

## Reading This Honestly

- The set is derived from [[RQ3 Control Coverage|RQ3]]'s step verdicts, so it inherits [[RQ3 Control Coverage|RQ3]]'s limits: one analyst's mapping, not independently rated; relevance decided per deployment; "bounded within limits" picks bound only to the stated limit.
- "Earliest" is judged on the paths as the G and S notes draw them. A path drawn with different steps could move a pick.
- For the Class D paths the one shot is the grant or the isolation. That stops the harm from leaving the grant; it does nothing about harm within it, which is [[RQ3 Control Coverage|RQ3]]'s residual and the point where H1b is weakest. An organization whose use case cannot tolerate that residual should, per NCSC, reconsider the use case rather than add controls [\[27\]](Sources.md#^s27).
- Each CIS safeguard carries its lowest Implementation Group from the v8.1 catalog [\[289\]](Sources.md#^s289). Of the fourteen in the minimum, five are IG1 (3.3 data access control lists, 6.2 access revoking, 6.3 MFA for externally exposed applications, 11.2 automated backups, 11.4 isolated recovery data), eight are IG2, and one is IG3: 6.8 role-based access control, which is load-bearing, since it is the CIS form of the grant that stops the direct-injection, intent-propagation, supplied-model backdoor, and tool-misuse paths. So an organization at IG1, what CIS calls essential cyber hygiene, has five of the fourteen CIS safeguards in the minimum, and the control that matters most for agents is the one CIS reserves for IG3.
