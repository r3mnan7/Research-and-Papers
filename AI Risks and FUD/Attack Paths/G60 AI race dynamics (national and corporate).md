# G60 AI race dynamics (national and corporate)

Back to [[RQ2 Screening Log]]

Path, lights, and scope call produced with AI assistance under the fixed method in the screening log. Quotes came through automated extraction; verify before citing.

**Harm (as claimed):** Competition pushes developers or states to deploy unsafe AI, up to catastrophe (Hendrycks et al.: actors "compel[led]… to deploy unsafe AIs or cede control to AIs").

**Path:**

| # | Step | Light | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Intense competitive pressure among developers or states | 🟢 | IASR 2026: "pace of development can create pressure to prioritise speed over risk management"; export controls cited |
| 2 | Competition causes a specific relaxation of safeguards or an unsafe deployment | 🔴 | The mechanism has been put in writing: OpenAI, "Updating our Preparedness Framework" (15 Apr 2025), https://openai.com/index/updating-our-preparedness-framework/: "If another frontier AI developer releases a high-risk system without comparable safeguards, we may adjust our requirements." No primary-documented instance of an actual race-driven relaxation was found (search was limited). |
| 3 | The unsafe system causes serious or catastrophic harm | 🔴 | Hendrycks, Mazeika, Woodside, arXiv 2306.12001 (2023) is argument, not demonstration |

**Weakest-link light:** 🔴

**In scope:** No. Downstream harms may involve security failures, but they belong to other concerns.

**Gaps:** This research did not search for documented cases of shortened safety testing. Those would be the key evidence for step 2.
