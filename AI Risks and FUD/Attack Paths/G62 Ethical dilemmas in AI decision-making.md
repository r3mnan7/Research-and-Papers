# G62 Ethical dilemmas in AI decision-making

Back to [[RQ2 Screening Log]]

Path, lights, and scope call produced with AI assistance under the fixed method in the screening log. Quotes came through automated extraction; verify before citing.

**Harm (as claimed):** Algorithmic decision systems make value-laden decisions (e.g., discriminatory risk scoring) that unfairly harm people.

**Path:**

| # | Step | Light | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | An organisation delegates a consequential decision to an algorithmic model | 🟢 | Netherlands Kamerstuk 31066 nr. 936 (7 Dec 2021), https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/kst-31066-936.pdf: Toeslagen risk-classification model |
| 2 | The model encodes a contested or discriminatory value choice | 🟢 | Same: nationality used as a risk indicator from Mar 2016 to Oct 2018 |
| 3 | Individuals are harmed by the decisions | 🟢 | Same letter: €2.75M AP fine for unlawful processing. "Ongekend onrecht," Kamerstuk 35 510 nr. 2, 17 Dec 2020, https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/kst-35510-2.pdf: "grondbeginselen van de rechtsstaat zijn geschonden" (fundamental principles of the rule of law were violated). |

**Weakest-link light:** 🟢

**In scope:** No. Unlawful processing is a lawfulness and fairness failure, not a confidentiality or integrity breach.

**Gaps:** Whether this counts as "AI" is settled by three primary sources that call the model "zelflerend" (self-learning): the AP report (blg-945542), "Ongekend onrecht" (p. 10), and the State Secretary's letter, Kamerstuk 31 066 nr. 683, 17 Jul 2020. Other cases (e.g., State v. Loomis) were not researched.
