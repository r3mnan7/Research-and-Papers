**Harm (as claimed):** (a) AI regulation imposes costly, unworkable compliance burdens and penalties. (b) Governance fails to keep pace, so harmful AI use goes unchecked.

**Path (a: burden):**

| # | Step | Light | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Regulators adopt AI-specific obligations | 🟢 | EU AI Act (Reg. 2024/1689), described in EPRS "Digital Omnibus on AI", PE 785.685 (Mar 2026), https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2026/785685/EPRS_ATA(2026)785685_EN.pdf. This research did not fetch the AI Act text itself. |
| 2 | Obligations exceed readiness (standards or tools not available) | 🟢 | Same: on 19 Nov 2025 the Commission proposed tying high-risk deadlines to standards availability (final dates 2 Dec 2027 and 2 Aug 2028) to "reduce unnecessary regulatory burden" |
| 3 | Organisations incur penalties or costs (alternative: innovation is suppressed) | 🔴 | The one AI-related penalty found does not stand. The Garante fined OpenAI €15M (Decision 755, 2 Nov 2024; press release 20 Dec 2024, https://www.garanteprivacy.it/home/docweb/-/docweb-display/print/10085432). The Rome Tribunal annulled it on 18 Mar 2026 (R.G. 4785/2025; the Garante's page cites judgment No. 4153/2026), https://dei.web.uniroma1.it/sites/default/files/allegati/2026-05/Trib_Roma_OpenAI_Garante_2026.pdf, on competence alone: OpenAI's Irish establishment made Ireland the lead authority ("stabilimento unico"). The court did not rule on the merits. No primary measurement of compliance cost found. The innovation-suppression alternative is 🔴. |
