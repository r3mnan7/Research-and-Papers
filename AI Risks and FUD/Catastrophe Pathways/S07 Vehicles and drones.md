# S07 Vehicles and drones: mass remote takeover of a fleet

Back to [[RQ2 Screening Log]] · [[Catastrophe Pathways]]

> [!info] Method
> Map the attack path, then light each step by evidence: 🟢 happened in the real world · 🟡 shown only in a lab or evaluation · 🔴 never shown. The scenario is only as real as its weakest step. Quotes came through automated extraction; verify before citing.

**Weakest-link light:** 🔴 Speculative

**Necessary security failure (in scope?):** In. Requires remote access and integrity failures in vehicle software or update channels.

## Attack path

| # | Step | Light | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Find a remote entry point into vehicles | 🟡 | Shown by researchers on real vehicles: 2015 Jeep recall of 1,424,519 vehicles over an open cellular port [\[191\]](../Sources.md#^s191). No malicious use reported. |
| 2 | Reach safety-critical controls | 🟡 | Same researcher demonstration [\[191\]](../Sources.md#^s191). |
| 3 | Take over many vehicles at once (e.g., via update channel) | 🔴 | No documented fleet-wide malicious takeover. U.S. rules restrict adversary-linked connected-vehicle software (MY2027) and hardware (MY2030) [\[192\]](../Sources.md#^s192). The BIS final rule was published 16 Jan 2025 (90 FR 5360; effective 17 Mar 2025); it "prohibits transactions involving Vehicle Connectivity System (VCS) hardware and covered software" linked to the PRC or Russia [\[195\]](../Sources.md#^s195). |
| 4 | AI materially enables the attack | 🔴 | No primary source found. |

## Also on record

- Commerce warned adversaries could "remotely manipulate cars on American roads" [\[193\]](../Sources.md#^s193); similar concern for drones [\[194\]](../Sources.md#^s194).

- UN Regulation No. 155 makes a cybersecurity management system a condition of vehicle type approval where it is adopted: an "exhaustive risk assessment for the vehicle type," "appropriate and sufficient testing," and post-registration monitoring reported "at least once a year" [\[196\]](../Sources.md#^s196).
- NHTSA's 2022 best practices are voluntary ("remain voluntary and non-binding"): "Any credential obtained from a single vehicle's computing platform should not provide access to other vehicles" [\[197\]](../Sources.md#^s197).
